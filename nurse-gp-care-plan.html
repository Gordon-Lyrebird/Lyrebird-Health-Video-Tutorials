How the nurse to GP care plan works

The nurse runs the appointment and the draft comes out of the conversation. The GP opens the same patient, sees the handover note first, adds their part and signs off. This covers both halves and the handover between them.

Video walkthrough · Loom

Why this workflow exists

In most practices we speak to, the care plan is already a two person job. The nurse runs the longer appointment, takes the observations, works through the conditions and the goals. The GP reviews it, adds the clinical decisions and signs off. That division is not new, and it is not something Lyrebird introduced.

What has usually been true is that the work gets typed twice. The nurse types their part into the plan. The GP then reads it, re-types their own section, and adds what came out of their consult. The handover in between is often verbal, or a note in the file, or the nurse catching the doctor between patients.

This changes none of who does what. The nurse still runs the groundwork and the GP still signs off. What changes is that each half is drafted from the conversation in the room, and the handover is a button rather than a catch up in the corridor.

This example is the CCMP, but the workflow is the same for all of them

Applies to any nurse led care plan

The video walks through a chronic condition management plan because it is the most common one. Every other nurse led document in the panel works the same way, including health assessments. Select a different item and the prompts change to match it. Everything after that, the draft, the handover note, the send to GP, the review and the sign off, is identical.

Part one: the nurse appointment

  1. From the nurse account, open Lyrebird from the patient's record in Best Practice.
  2. On the right hand side, search for the care plan you need. Typing CCMP brings up the initial 965.
  3. Press Start listening.
  4. Two things are now on screen.
    • Reminders of the usual questions and the structure of a CCMP, so the nurse has the key points in front of them.
    • Information already pulled through by the two way integration: medical history, patient details, social history and Medicare details.
  5. Run the appointment as you normally would. What Lyrebird needs from the conversation is the SMART goals, the plan for the patient, and any handover notes you would usually pass to the GP.
  6. Press Stop listening.
  7. The consult notes generate on the left. On the right, every section of the care plan populates.
  8. Click through each section and review it, including what was discussed last time against what was discussed today. This is the draft the GP will see.
  9. At the bottom is the handover note, carrying everything the nurse raised that the GP needs to know.

The handover

  1. If the GP is using Lyrebird, press Send to GP.
  2. The clinic is notified.
  3. When the doctor opens Lyrebird from that patient's record, the care plan is there for them to finalise.
Nothing is re-keyed

The plan does not get emailed, printed or read out between patients. It moves with the patient record.

Part two: the GP appointment

  1. From the GP account, open Lyrebird from the patient's record. The GP CCMP shows as ready for review.
  2. Press Start listening. The nurse's draft opens, and the handover notes are the first thing on screen.
  3. Work through the rest of the plan in the consultation. Medication changes and script renewals, then the referrals the nurse raised.
  4. Add anything further. In the video the GP adds an exercise physiologist to support the activity goal the nurse set.
  5. Press Stop listening. The GP gets their own set of consult notes, generated from their consultation.
  6. Go through each section you spoke about.
  7. At the bottom, check the consent boxes a GP CCMP requires.
  8. Save into Best Practice.

Where it lands

  1. Go to Correspondence Out in Best Practice.
  2. Press F5, or use the view button and refresh.
  3. The completed care plan is there.

What Lyrebird fills in for you

Drafted from the consultation recording

Both halves are drafts. The nurse reviews theirs before sending, and the GP reviews the whole plan before the consent boxes are ticked and it is saved.

  • Medical history, patient details, social history and Medicare details, through the two way integration.
  • The prompts that match the structure of the item you selected.
  • The SMART goals and plan discussed in the appointment.
  • The handover note for the GP.
  • A separate set of consult notes for each clinician.

What this changes

  • The same division of work, without the plan being typed twice.
  • The handover is a button, and the GP sees the nurse's notes first.
  • Each clinician gets their own consult notes from their own appointment.
  • Patient information comes through from Best Practice rather than being re-entered.
  • The finished plan saves into Correspondence Out.
